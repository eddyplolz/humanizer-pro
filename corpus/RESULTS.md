## Separation by register

AUROC is the chance a random machine document ranks above a random human
one (0.5 = no better than a coin, 1.0 = always). It needs no threshold.
Ties count half, and a blocked document ranks above every score, as it is
flagged at every threshold. The mean risk gap is machine mean risk minus
human mean risk, in score points; it ignores blocks.

## Reading grade by register

Flesch-Kincaid grade of each document, test split. It approximates a
US school grade and can fall below zero for very plain text. It is
reported only and never adds points to the risk score. AUROC above
0.5 means machine text reads at a higher grade than human text in
that register; below 0.5, a lower one. The grade is rough on text
with few full stops, such as lists, code, and old books, and it is
meant for English only.

## Honest limits

- Register mapping: Stack Exchange answers (hobby, language, and
  workplace sites) → chat, English Wikipedia articles at their last
  pre-cutoff revision → wiki, public-domain prose (Strunk 1918, Emerson,
  Thoreau, Twain) and PERSUADE 2.0 student essays (US grades 6–12,
  written 2010–2020) → essay, CC-News web articles (at most three
  per outlet, dated before the cutoff) → news, Python Enhancement
  Proposals → docs.
- The news pool is crawled web text. The build drops articles with
  consent banners, newsletter prompts, and similar page furniture, but
  captions, datelines, and link lists can remain, and invisible
  characters from the page stay in the text. A block on them counts as
  a false positive, because the same characters reach the tool when a
  person pastes from the page.
- A Wikipedia entry is a whole article (bounded at 4,000 words), so
  it is many editors' text; a Stack Exchange entry is one answer, with
  code and quoted blocks removed. Answers are top-voted, so the chat
  slice leans toward careful writers.
- Machine text comes from RAID (2023 generators), WildChat (GPT-3.5
  and GPT-4 replies), and answers from current Claude models to 60
  published prompts (scripts/generate_machine.py). The Claude answers
  are the only machine essays. Their text is not published, so a
  rebuild generates new answers and its numbers can differ slightly.
  The catch rate describes those models only; tells change between
  model generations.
- Era confound: about half the human essays are a century old, while
  machine essays are modern, so a gap there is partly era, not
  authorship. The essay slice holds century-old prose and modern student
  essays side by side. The news, wiki, chat, and docs slices compare
  contemporaries more closely.
- Every pool is public and rebuildable from the manifest's pointers
  (`corpus.py fetch` or the named `build-*` command); every document
  comes from a public source.
- Registers are measured separately because rates differ by register;
  quoting the overall number alone misrepresents the tool.
- Periods are measured separately because a human set frozen before the
  cutoff drifts away from today's writing; the 2018–2022 band is the one
  closest to the prose the tool meets, and the one to watch.
