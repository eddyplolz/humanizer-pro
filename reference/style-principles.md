# Style Principles

Compact operational guidance drawn from William Strunk Jr.'s public-domain *The Elements of Style*
and adapted for Humanizer Pro. Use this by default for substantial style edits. Load
`reference/elements-of-style-1918.md` only when the user explicitly asks for Strunk/Elements detail or
when a deep style pass needs the full source.

## How to Use This Reference

Use these principles to clarify prose after the artifact and AI-tell passes. Do not use them as a
license to flatten a writer's voice, remove every passive construction, or force a house style where
the target publication already has one.

## Core Checklist

1. **Make every word do work.** Cut throat-clearing, duplicated meaning, padded transitions, and
   preambles that announce the sentence instead of adding information.
2. **Prefer concrete language.** Replace vague nouns and abstractions with the actual person, action,
   object, number, or consequence.
3. **Use active voice where it improves force or clarity.** Keep passive voice when the actor is
   unknown, unimportant, or the object is the topic.
4. **Put statements in positive form.** Say what happened, what is true, or what the writer believes.
   Avoid negative phrasing used only to hedge or soften.
5. **Keep related words together.** Move modifiers next to what they modify. Recast dangling
   participles and ambiguous "this/these" references.
6. **Make the paragraph the unit of composition.** Each paragraph should have one job. Split when the
   paragraph changes topic; merge when two fragments do the same work.
7. **Put emphasis where readers feel it.** End sentences and paragraphs on the word or fact that
   matters, not on a trailing qualifier or vague summary phrase.
8. **Use parallel form for parallel ideas.** Align grammatical shape only where the ideas are truly
   coordinate. Do not create fake triplets or slogans.
9. **Avoid monotonous loose sentences.** Vary structure when several sentences have the same
   subject-verb-tail rhythm.
10. **Prefer plain punctuation.** Fix comma splices, dangling fragments, and serial punctuation only
    when they interfere with clarity or violate the target style.

## Interaction With the Nine Tell Families

- Style work often overlaps Family 3 (filler), Family 4 (diction), Family 5 (syntax), and Family 6
  (padding). Use the family labels when reporting a full audit.
- Strunk's "omit needless words" supports the Density score, but Restraint still controls the edit.
- Concrete language is not fake specificity. If a fact is not known, flag the gap instead of inventing
  a number, name, or source.
- Active voice is useful, not mandatory. Rewriting every passive sentence can make neutral or academic
  prose worse.

## Outdated or Advisory Rules

Treat early twentieth-century prescriptions as advisory, not absolute. In particular:

- Keep the target publication's citation, punctuation, and spelling conventions.
- Do not rewrite idiom solely because Strunk disliked the phrase.
- Do not treat a single rule violation as an AI tell. Look for density, co-occurrence, and actual
  damage to clarity.
- Do not impose old-fashioned diction. The goal is clear modern prose, not period pastiche.

## Style-Edit Output

For a simple style request, return only the edited text unless a source or meaning risk appears. For a
full style audit, group notes under:

- Clarity and concrete language
- Sentence force and emphasis
- Paragraph unity
- Needless words removed
- Restraint: what stayed because it already worked
